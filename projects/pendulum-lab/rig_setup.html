Pendulum rig: setup guide

Dimensions match configs/sim.yaml: arm radius 7 cm, bob marker 35 cm below the pivot, and a camera scale of 8 px/cm at the pendulum plane. This is a diagram, not a photo. Wire colours and part shapes are approximate.

Never power the servo from the UNO's 5V pin, and never join the UNO's 5V to the charger's +5 V. Connect the grounds only.

Build order

  1. Centre the servo first. Send 1500 µs, then screw the arm on pointing straight down.
  2. Glue or screw the SG90 to the front face of a wooden block. Put the block on the table edge and weigh or clamp it down, so the arm swings just in front of the edge.
  3. Arm: a popsicle stick with a hole 7 cm from the servo shaft. Push a smooth nail through it, pointing towards the camera.
  4. Rod: drill a hole slightly larger than the nail near the top, and hang it on the nail. Add a tape collar so it can't slide off. It should swing freely for more than 20 s from 30°.
  5. Bob: 30–50 g of nuts on a bolt through the rod. Tape a 5 × 5 cm cardboard vane below it.
  6. Markers: matte (not glossy) paper circles. Green (≈1.5 cm) on the nail head at the pivot, magenta (≈2.5 cm) on the bob, 35 cm from the pivot.
  7. Backdrop: a matte black card taped to the table edge, hanging behind the pendulum. For test E1a, tape the two-page protractor (25 cm, ±45°) so its crosshair is exactly behind the pivot nail and its 0° line hangs vertical (check it against the resting rod). Run every wire along the backdrop edges, outside the swing area.
  8. Light gate: the white LED on the backdrop at vane height, shining towards the camera, and the photoresistor on a stand in front, so the vane cuts the beam at the bottom of the swing.
  9. Latency LED: red, fixed to the backdrop's top-right corner, inside the camera frame.
  10. Laptop: screen facing the rig, about 75 cm away, raised on a stool or books so the servo appears near the top of the image. Check this with Webcam's view.
  11. Lamp: a bright DC LED lamp above and to the front-left, angled so the markers don't glare. The camera needs about 4–8 ms exposure.

Parts

  • Owned: UNO R3, SG90, breadboard, 100 µF capacitor, photoresistor, 10 kΩ, 2× 220 Ω, white and red LEDs, jumper wires, USB cable, laptop webcam.
  • Household: wooden block, popsicle stick, smooth nail, wooden ruler or stick, nuts and bolt, cardboard, black card, tape, stool or books, desk lamp, spare 5 V ≥ 2 A phone charger (with its cable cut).
  • Print: docs/print/protractor_R250_A4x2.pdf, a 25 cm radius, ±45° protractor with 1° ticks on 2 A4 sheets. Print at actual size on A4, then check that the horizontal and vertical check bars are equal in length. Exactly 10 cm is ideal but not required, because angles survive uniform scaling. The static test only needs ±45°, and 1 mm of alignment error at 25 cm radius is about 0.23°.

Safety

  • Keep your face and hands out of the swing zone while the servo is powered.
  • Unplug the charger before rewiring. Check polarity before connecting it.
