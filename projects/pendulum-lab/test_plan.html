General rules:

  • Every experiment is a script plus a config under experiments/. Every run is logged by RunLogger.
  • Hardware trials of competing methods are interleaved in randomised order, so that drift (temperature, pivot wear, battery) does not favour any one method.
  • Hardware results report every run, plus the median and a 95% bootstrap confidence interval.
  • Simulation results report the median, the IQR, a 95% bootstrap confidence interval, and a paired Wilcoxon test across seeds.

Hardware session routine (every session)

The Sony α7 IV streams for only about 20–25 min before switching itself off (heat), and each restart can move or re-zoom the view. So every session:

  1. experiments/check_calibration.py: about 15 s. Only if it prints FAIL, run experiments/calibrate_rig.py --rest-wait 35 --write, which also stores the servo's power-up home on the board.
  2. experiments/e1c_latency.py --toggles 30: about 20 s. Update camera.capture_latency only if it moved by more than about 5 ms.
  3. At most about 12 min of trials, then let the camera rest.

Chain the steps so that a failed check stops them. Use cmd1 || cmd2 for "recalibrate on FAIL", and never pipe a check's output before testing its exit status: a pipe hides it (this happened once on 2026-09-28).

Settle rule: before and after every trial the servo is released (limp) until the camera sees the pendulum at rest, then re-engaged at φ = 0. A holding SG90 keeps pumping the swing through its dead band.

E1. Sensor validation

  • E1a, static angle: print docs/print/protractor_R250_A4x2.pdf (radius 250 mm, ±45°, 1° ticks, 2 × A4). It is generated by scripts/make_protractor.py.

    • Print at actual size on A4. The horizontal and vertical check bars must be equal in length; exactly 100 mm is ideal but not required, because angles survive uniform scaling. Fix the protractor behind the pendulum with its crosshair on the pivot and its 0° line along the resting rod.

    • Tape the rod along the −45°, −30°, −15°, 0°, 15°, 30° and 45° lines, recording 60 frames at each. Compare the camera angle with the sheet angle.

    • Reference uncertainty: about 1 mm of alignment at 250 mm, which is ≈ 0.23°.

    • Pass: R-S3.

    • Guided procedure (no keyboard): experiments/e1a_static_angle.py --guided.

      1. Check that the protractor's centre cross is on the pivot and that the sheet has not moved.
      2. Hold the ruler with a fingertip so its centre line lies along the printed line. Keep your hand below the pink dot and out of the camera's view of both dots.
      3. Order: 0°, +15°, +30°, +45°, then −15°, −30°, −45°. "+" means the ruler's tip points to the right as seen from the front (the camera's view).
      4. At each line, hold still. When the camera sees the ruler still within ±6° of the line for 1.5 s, the red LED turns ON while it records (about 2 s). When it turns OFF, move to the next line.
      5. An angle not held still within 90 s is skipped.

      The result reports the RMS error and the mean offset (for example, a sheet slightly rotated), with the RMS after removing that offset.

  • E1b, period: a light gate (LED + photoresistor) sits at the bottom of the swing, with a 5 cm cardboard vane on the bob so the beam stays blocked long enough for the slow photoresistor (see risk below). Record 3 free decays from 30°. Compare period estimates from gate timestamps (board clock) and camera zero-crossings. Pass: R-S4.
    • Before E1b: re-check the gate levels under the final lighting. The LED panel and the new ISO/room setup change the light the photoresistor sees; the 720/790 thresholds were set on 2026-09-28 before the panel was used.
      • experiments/check_gate.py --params runs/<e3>/params.yaml: the controller swings the pendulum up to 25°, the servo is released, and A0 is polled at about 250 Hz during the free decay.
      • Clear level = 95th percentile; one dip per passage (checked against the camera's passage count). The shallowest dip, from the fastest pass, sets blocked = clear − 0.35 × depth and clear threshold = clear − 0.15 × depth.
      • Add --write to store the thresholds in rig.yaml.
      • On the morning's recording, the rule gives 755/810 (hand-set: 720/790); all 30 passes are detectable.
    • Hands-free E1b: experiments/e1b_period.py --pump runs/<e3>/params.yaml. Each repeat swings the pendulum up to --release-deg, releases the servo, and records the free decay; nobody has to hold the rod.
    • As built, the rig has no vane: the ruler blocks the beam itself (see §5 of the guide).
  • E1c, latency: the board toggles an LED in the camera view at random intervals and reports each toggle time over serial. The host detects LED state changes in frames. Clock offset comes from ping round-trips. The measurement is repeated 200 times. Pass: R-S5 and R-S6.
  • Before E1: experiments/calibrate_rig.py --write (servo axis, scale, roll, servo trim from the markers).
  • Scripts: e1a_static_angle.py (interactive), e1b_period.py (gate edges and camera θ; the board clock is mapped to host time with a rate-fitted ping clock map), e1c_latency.py (LED region found automatically; prints the capture_latency for rig.yaml). All accept --fake for a dry run.
  • Risk: CdS photoresistors respond in about 10–30 ms (typical value, to be measured). This is why E1b uses the period, in which constant delays cancel, and not absolute crossing times.

E2. Simulation identification campaign

  • Setup: 3 "true" parameter sets (nominal, high friction, short pendulum) × 50 seeds × 4 protocols:
    • free decay;
    • chirp;
    • random excitation drawn from the library;
    • sequential Bayesian D-optimal design.
  • Budget: each protocol gets the same total experiment time.
  • Metrics:
    • relative parameter error;
    • 95% interval coverage;
    • held-out prediction NRMSE at 1, 3 and 5 s;
    • log-det of the posterior covariance vs cumulative experiment time.
  • Pass: R-I1, R-I2 and R-I3. This is also the simulation test of H1.
  • As run (2026-09-27, experiments/e2_sim_campaign.py): 20 seeds per set (not 50: the numbers were stable between 5 and 20 seeds and the run takes 12 min), each seed perturbing all true parameters by 10%; 30 s budget in 5 s segments with a fit after each; held-out set per unit = multisine 15 s + free decay from 25° + burst; metric also reported relative to the noise floor (true parameters on the same held-out data). Free decay starts from 20° with the servo held. Candidates must also leave the swing ≤ 45° at the segment end (terminal constraint, added after one R-I3 violation). Results and the H1/R-I1 discussion: engineering log 2026-09-27 (M3).

E3. Hardware identification campaign

  • Setup: ≥ 8 runs per protocol, in interleaved random order.
  • Held-out data: a fixed set of 5 validation excitations, recorded separately. It is never used for fitting.
  • Metrics: prediction NRMSE on the held-out set; servo parameters with their uncertainty (R-A3).
  • Tests: H1 on hardware.
  • As implemented (2026-09-28): experiments/e3_campaign.py
    • Held-out set: --validation --params <e3 params> records 3 excitations once, each as its own run: a multisine (15 s), a free decay from 25° (swung up by the controller, then φ held at 0; 10 s) and a burst (12 s). These are the same shapes as in simulation (E2).
    • Campaign: --validation-runs <3 runs> --blocks N --order-seed S. Each block is one chirp unit and one D-optimal unit, in a shuffled order. A unit starts from rest and has a 30 s budget in 5 s segments. After each segment the model is fitted to the unit's data so far and scored on the held-out set; D-optimal picks the next segment from the library, with the predicted-safety filter (≤ 60°, segment end ≤ 45°). A measured |θ| > 60° stops the unit.
    • Real time: the laptop's fit and design take 1–5 s while the pendulum keeps moving. The record is padded with "held at the last command" for that pause, so the command grid stays on real time. Frames from the pauses are excluded from the fit: otherwise D-optimal, which thinks longer, would get free extra data. That bias was seen in a dry run: pauses of ≈ 5 s against ≈ 1 s for the chirp.
    • Analysis: python analysis/e3_hw.py. Noise floor = held-out NRMSE of one fit to all units pooled (v1.1). Time to floor per unit; H1 = median per-block ratio of D-optimal time to chirp time ≤ 0.5; paired Wilcoxon. Writes docs/results/e3_hardware.md and docs/figures/e3_hw_nrmse.png.
    • Size: 8 blocks, i.e. ≥ 8 runs per protocol as planned. About 3 min per block, so 3–4 blocks per camera session.

E5. Closed-loop control

  • Controllers:

    1. energy-based controller with the actively identified model;
    2. the same controller with hand-measured parameters;
    3. hand-tuned PID on the amplitude envelope.

  • Setup: 3 controllers × 3 target amplitudes × ≥ 5 runs.

  • Metrics: time to reach ±2°, steady-state RMS amplitude error, overshoot, servo effort, safety stops.

  • Pass: R-C1 and R-C2.

  • Script: experiments/e5_control.py (simulation by default; --hardware for the rig). Amplitude = |θ| turning points (the energy-equivalent amplitude ripples while the pivot moves). The H2 ablation (energy_naive: no servo model, no prediction to now) runs alongside. Simulation results: engineering log 2026-09-27 (M5).

  • Hardware, as run (2026-09-28): one frozen configuration, "C4" in the report. It has:

    • the joint-fit parameters runs/20260928-194003_e3_identify;
    • rig.yaml ekf (sigma_t 0.010, r_phi 0.015) and control (low-pass 3 Hz, integral band 6°, k_integral 1.0);
    • 30 s trials, servo cap 10°, slew limit 200°/s.

    Every trial is settled before and after (see the session routine), and the full servo command log is written.

    • R-C1: 15/25/35° with the identified energy controller: 12/12 (95% CI 74–100%).
    • To show ≥ 80% at 95% confidence, about 20 consecutive passes are needed; the H2 sessions below add 9.

  • H2 campaign on hardware (planned): energy_identified against energy_naive, the same controller without the servo model and without prediction to now.

    • Design: balanced blocks. Each session runs all 6 (controller × target) combinations once, in a shuffled order, which fits the camera limit (about 8–9 min). 3 sessions with order seeds 10, 11 and 12 give 9 pairs:

      python experiments/e5_control.py --hardware --params runs/20260928-194003_e3_identify/params.yaml             --controllers energy_identified energy_naive --targets 15 25 35 --repeats 1 --duration 30 --order-seed 10

    • Test: H2 = at least a 30% reduction in the median RMS amplitude error, paired by target and session, with a paired Wilcoxon test.

    • A naive trial that ends in a safety stop is reported as such, and counts as an R-C1 failure for that controller.

  • Results report: python analysis/hw_results.py rebuilds docs/results/e5_hardware.md (per configuration: R-C1 with exact intervals, trial table, servo statistics, H2 pairs), docs/results/e5_hardware_trials.csv and docs/figures/e5_hw_amplitude.png from all hardware runs. Run it after every session and commit the output: runs/ is not committed.

E6. Perturbations

  • Add 10–20 g to the bob.
  • Shorten the rod by 5 cm.
  • Dim the lighting.
  • Tap the bob mid-run.

Measure the degradation of E5 metrics, then re-identify and re-measure.

E7. Sim-to-real gap

Run the same controller gains and the same excitations in simulation (identified parameters) and on hardware. Report the trajectory NRMSE and metric deltas.

Ablations (on E3/E5 data)

  1. No servo model (φ = φ_cmd).
  2. No latency compensation.
  3. EKF replaced by finite differences.
  4. D-optimal design from the point estimate vs posterior-averaged.
  5. Safety constraint off (simulation only; count the violations it avoided).
  6. Frame rate subsampled 30 → 15 fps.
